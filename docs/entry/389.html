Campamento militar de Toniná (Mexican Army camp at the Toniná ruins, Ocosingo)

Ocosingo, Chiapas, Mexico · Mexico and Central America · Mexican Army (SEDENA), established during the post-1994 militarisation of the Zapatista conflict zone · founded 1995 (c.1995–late 1990s (temporary camp; present status unknown))

Evidence: Unverified Deliberate reuse of an Indigenous place

What lies beneath

Toniná, the Classic Maya acropolis-city of Ocosingo (Popol/Toniná dynasty, AD c.300–900). A June 1996 international civil observer delegation report (LAI/Otros Mundos Chiapas) recorded: "Hay un campamento militar en las ruinas de Toniná, rancho Majosil y Nuevo Jerusalén, con 2 mil soldados", with armoured vehicles, helicopters and light tanks. No INAH report on physical impact located.

Concealment (documented)

Alleged: occupation of an INAH-protected Maya site by a 2,000-strong army camp during counter-insurgency deployment (1996 observer report). No destruction, repatriation or litigation documented; not corroborated by INAH or HRW sources opened.
