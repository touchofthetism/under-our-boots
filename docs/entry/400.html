Isla Quiriquina naval complex — Escuela de Grumetes 'Alejandro Navarrete Cisterna', Fuerte Quiriquina Sur and coastal batteries Rondizzoni, Miller and Maruri

Isla Quiriquina, Bahía de Concepción, Talcahuano, Región del Biobío, Chile · South America · Chilean Navy; island bought by the Chilean State in 1896 and handed to the Navy; fort battery 1898/1908; school buildings from 1910, school installed 1921; coastal batteries 1928 · founded 1896 (1896–present (coastal artillery disused 1952–1957; school active))

Evidence: Probable Deliberate reuse of an Indigenous place

What lies beneath

Per the Navy's own Revista de Marina (2018), the island's 'primer propietario' was the cacique Leochengo, and Agustín de Zárate (1555) described an island in the bay 'dedicada a sus ídolos, donde hay un gran templo', which the author identifies as Quiriquina; Mapuche-Lafkenche sacred island by chronicle rather than by excavation. No archaeological find on the naval grounds is reported in the source.

Relationship

Deliberate reuse of an Indigenous place

whole Indigenous-owned/sacred island taken as a naval school and fortress; pre-contact occupation known from chronicles, not from documented excavation

Flags

Land seizure / displacement; Restricted access

Access to the island is controlled by the Navy (the source notes this has protected its fossil beds from looting); no destruction, litigation or Indigenous claim documented in the source.
