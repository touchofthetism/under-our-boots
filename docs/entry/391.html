Presidio de San Miguel de Horcasitas

San Miguel de Horcasitas, Sonora, Mexico · Mexico and Central America · Spanish Crown (Nueva España), founded 1749 under Governor Diego Ortiz Parrilla; moved to Pitic/Hermosillo in the 1780s · founded 1749 (1749–c.1780s (garrison transferred); town persists)

Evidence: Probable Deliberate reuse of an Indigenous place

What lies beneath

Lands and settlement site of the Seri pueblos of Nuestra Señora del Pópulo and Los Ángeles on the Río San Miguel: a UNAM Estudios de Historia Novohispana study states the presidio was founded on land taken from the Seri pueblos, whose people "fueron los primeros pobladores del paraje que luego ocupó el presidio de San Miguel", and that the lands "pertenecían a dichos naturales". The Seri reductions were Jesuit mission pueblos (c.1700) on Seri ancestral territory; no excavated pre-Columbian site is reported.

Relationship

Deliberate reuse of an Indigenous place

presidio placed on the site and lands of Seri mission pueblos; pre-contact occupation of the exact spot not archaeologically documented

Flags

Destroyed

Documented dispossession: Seris of Pópulo and Los Ángeles displaced from their lands to make way for the presidio and Spanish settlers (1749), a trigger of the Seri uprising; no litigation or repatriation.
