Kahoʻolawe (Navy bombing range; Kahoʻolawe Island Reserve)

Hawaii, US · Hawaii and Alaska · US Navy (Army use from the 1930s; title to Navy 1953) · founded 1941 (1941–1990 bombing; UXO clearance 1997–2004; returned to the State 1994/2003)

Episode 2 — Kahoʻolawe — publishedEvidence: Confirmed Deliberate reuse of an Indigenous place

What lies beneath

Entire 28,800-acre sacred island (kinolau of Kanaloa; puʻuhonua): 544 recorded sites and >2,000 features at NRHP listing (18 Mar 1981); nearly 3,000 sites/features inventoried by 2004; Hakioawa village; Puʻu Mōiwi adze quarry; shrines; petroglyphs

Relationship

Deliberate reuse of an Indigenous place

whole sacred island; overlaps documented sites island-wide

Flags

Destroyed; Restricted access; Land seizure / displacement; Litigation / inquiry; Concealment (documented)

Destroyed (bombing 1941–90; Operation Sailor Hat 1965: three 500-ton TNT blasts); Restricted access (25% of the island, 6,692 acres, still uncleared and unsafe without escort); Land seizure (1941/1953); Litigation (PKO suit 1976; 1977 court order for an EIS and site inventory; 1980 consent decree; Title X 1993); Concealment (documented): the Navy's 1972 EIS said bombing had 'slightly improved' the island's ecosystems; the Navy cleared 75% of the surface against a 100% commitment and 9–10% to four feet against 30%; a deputy AG testified the State had sufficient grounds to sue in 2004 but did not
